What the tape says

    Live zones

    Supply and demand bands from 4-hour swing pivots.

    Data. Crypto.com Exchange public feed. Gold is priced through PAXG (Pax Gold), a token redeemable for one fine troy ounce of London Good Delivery bullion, so the quote is dollars per ounce and trades around the clock. Cross-check at the time of capture: PAXG at 4,593.31 against GLD's 415.26 close implies 0.0904 oz of gold per GLD share, which matches the fund's actual bullion backing — two independent feeds agreeing.

    The window is short, and that is a hard limit. The feed returns 50 candles per request, so the 15-minute chart spans about 12 hours and the 4-hour chart about 8 days. Zones are drawn from that 8-day structure only — older levels that still matter are simply not visible here. EMAs are 9 and 21 periods for the same reason: a 200-period average needs four times the history this feed will hand over in one call.

    How the zones are drawn. A swing pivot is a bar whose high or low exceeds every bar two periods either side. Pivots within 0.6 ATR are clustered, and each cluster becomes a band 0.6 ATR wide around its mean, so no zone can widen into a meaningless block. “Tests” counts later bars that traded into the band.
